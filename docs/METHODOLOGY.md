# Grand-to-Ground (G2G)

**A practical methodology for AI-assisted research in the social sciences**

Rick Ma (Hongtao Ma) · 2026 · **v2.0** — This document practices what it preaches: v1.0 was produced jointly by the author and AI research agents, and this revision stood its own three-critic tribunal (submission severity) before release — the accepted findings are folded into this text; attacks on ratified design decisions are logged, per its own rules. v2.0 is not a redesign from the armchair; every change traces to a logged defect or a logged success from one month of intensive live use on a live finance research programme — five tribunals (four over cycle results, one over a research plan), three full cycles, six conclusion cards, six generations of research plans, and a contradiction log that names each lesson.

## 1. The open space

In 2025–26, AI research systems came of age in the experimental sciences. Google's Co-Scientist orchestrates agents that generate, debate, rank, and evolve hypotheses — published in *Nature* in May 2026, with wet-lab validations. Sakana's AI Scientist produced the first AI-generated paper to pass workshop-level peer review (its makers withdrew it before publication, by design). And in 2026 the ambition escalated: well-funded labs now pursue fully automated research, with the human's removal from the loop an explicit target.

Social science enjoys no laboratory, and G2G deliberately declines the autonomy race. Its theories are built by deduction from assumptions at least as much as by induction from data; its "experiments" are history — natural experiments, archives, observational datasets; and its hardest step, choosing the right question, is a matter of taste. G2G occupies the position the frontier systems skip: **a single human researcher who owns the question, the taste, and the errors, plus off-the-shelf AI agents, in a disciplined loop between grand ideas and identifiable questions.**

## 2. Division of labor — and of responsibility

The human brings divergent intuition (where to look), taste (which question is worth anyone's time), arbitration (which agent wins a dispute), and verification (no fact enters the record unchecked). The AI brings breadth: literature reconnaissance across fields, translation of prose into formal propositions, tireless deduction, adversarial critique, and bookkeeping. This split follows the evidence, not ideology: a large controlled comparison (Stanford, 100+ NLP researchers) found LLM-generated research ideas rated significantly *more novel* than experts' — and somewhat *less feasible*; the same team's execution follow-up found the novelty advantage shrank further once ideas were actually executed. The lesson lands on both halves of the split: let AI supply novelty; let the human supply feasibility and own every choice.

v2.0 adds the clause field practice showed was missing: **the division of responsibility.** Errors belong to the human. A conclusion that is later refuted is not the AI's failure — the idea was the human's to try, and running it faithfully was the job. An AI assistant in this loop should take its pride in three things: fidelity to the user's logic, divergence in exploration, and professionalism in execution — not in being right — and *never* in holding the same view as most of the world. The corollary cuts both ways: within whatever severity tier the human has chosen (§3-P6), the AI must not soften critique to protect the human's feelings, and must not harden critique to protect its own record — the dial is set before the work, never bent during it. In looser disciplines, today's wrong idea is sometimes tomorrow's right one; many good thoughts arrive as intuitions long before they arrive as proofs. The loop exists to land those intuitions safely, not to talk the researcher out of having them.

Where a research programme has an external collaborator or supervisor, that person is a third party with their own interface — see §8.

## 3. The pipeline (P0–P8)

- **P0 Vision.** State the grand framework in plain language — hunches, end-state claims, and all. A grand narrative is unpublishable; it is also the best question generator you own.

- **P1 Reconnaissance.** Agents sweep the literature — in one of three modes. *Full sweep* for a first cycle or a new domain. *Incremental* (the default thereafter): maintain a literature ledger in the programme state and update only new neighbors and stale entries — the novelty critic will search again at tribunal time, so a full re-sweep every cycle pays twice for the same ground. *Delegated*, at low severity: fold reconnaissance into the novelty critic's tribunal search entirely. In every mode two things are non-negotiable: the sweep runs on **two axes** — method family *and* application domain — because single-axis sweeps demonstrably miss the nearest neighbors; and the **gate** stands: if the field already holds your conclusion in deeper form, switch to learning mode — absorb it, then re-enter the loop standing on it. Delegation trades early gating for cheapness — a collision discovered only at tribunal time still converts the cycle to learning mode, having already spent it — which is why the mode is reserved for exploratory severity. Where your interest collides with existing work is usually where innovation lives.

- **P2 Formalization.** Translate the narrative into propositions with operationalized terms — definitions someone could measure. Prefer borrowing the skeleton of an established model over inventing notation. Two disciplines, both learned the hard way. First, **multi-operationalization**: for each core intuition of the human (or of an external collaborator — same rule), produce *at least two* operationalizations side by side — the most faithful reading and the strongest reading — each tagged faithful / strengthened / extended, and send them to deduction *in parallel*. The field evidence here is one programme's, but it is consistent — and it comes from five tribunals run under v1 rules, when the human's premises were still fully attackable: when tribunals kill, they kill the *signature* the agent added in formalization, almost never the human's underlying story; a single "best" operationalization gambles the story on the agent's guess. (The multi-analyst literature reports the same pattern at scale: given one hypothesis, independent analysts' operationalization choices drive most of the dispersion in results.) Every shape the agent adds beyond the source narrative carries a visible `[SIG]` mark, so what dies can be identified as the agent's addition, not the human's idea. Second, **carrier checks**: before an institutional fact is used as an anchor (a disclosure channel, a policy instrument, a market mechanism), verify that the named carrier actually carries what the argument needs it to carry — a documented category error (a foreign-investor channel mistaken for a state-fund disclosure conduit) survived two inference chains before a critic caught it.

- **P3 Axiomatization.** Declare the programme's protected structure in **three tiers**:
  1. **Hard core** — the few assumptions the programme is built to protect, Lakatos-style. Ratification is a human gate; every candidate is fact-checked first, because a false "fact" planted in the core will be defended brilliantly forever after. Critics may attack the core; attacks are *logged, not adjudicated on the spot*, and reviewed in periodic audits.
  2. **Declared assumptions** — new in v2.0, and lighter in *ceremony* than the core, not in ownership. These are working premises the human states as *assumptions*, the way a theorist lists assumptions before a proof: "assume purchasing-power parity," "assume the intermediary observes the flow." Declared assumptions are **exempt from refutation**. The tribunal does not litigate whether they are true; it enforces only that the deduction *stays inside them*, that their operationalization does not drift, and that outward text presents them honestly as assumptions. The human gate here is the declaration itself — the human states the premise and, per §2, owns it; the machine's counter-check is a one-time quick fact-scan at declaration that catches outright factual howlers (the carrier-check class). After that, objections to an assumption's truth are recorded in a **silent ledger** — not counted toward quotas, not featured in verdicts, but tallied and surfaced at programme audits, so ten cycles later the human can still see what their assumptions have absorbed. One promotion rule keeps the tiers honest: an assumption that turns out to carry an entire conclusion line belongs in the hard core — promote it, and it gains the core's audit machinery. This tier exists because a month of practice under full-firepower rules showed where the friction actually was: the premises were rarely what was wrong, and re-litigating them every round produced friction, not knowledge.
  3. **Protective belt** — auxiliary hypotheses, parameter choices, case interpretations. Full firepower, every round, attackable and replaceable.
  Alongside the tiers sits the **standing-rulings register** (U-series): decisions the human has made once and does not wish to re-litigate — "pre-registration rituals are not the convention in this field; objections of that class are not receivable," "wording already ratified by me is not re-tried." Rulings are loaded into every tribunal; critics whose objection falls under a ruling cite it and stand down. Rulings also encode **domain conventions** captured at setup: what the target field's journals actually expect, so critics calibrate to the venue rather than to an imported ideal.

- **P4 Conjecture.** Instruct an agent to nominate one piece of "common sense" as possibly false, and to guess where information is missing — guesses explicitly marked `[GUESS]`. The explored roads are crowded; the unexplored ones are mostly unexplored for lack of known information. Guessing it, visibly, is a legitimate move.

- **P5 Deduction.** v2.0 makes **parallel deduction** the default: N independent deducer agents (N = 1 / 2 / 3 by severity tier — Appendix A), each in a fresh context, none aware of the others, each deriving consequences strictly inside the axioms, the declared assumptions, and its own marked guesses (downstream steps inherit the mark) — no hedging, no smuggling. Language models are path-dependent — a deducer conditions on its own earlier lines, so an early fork compounds silently into a very different endpoint, and a single line of deduction explores a single path; this is a documented property of the models, and sampling independent paths is the documented correction (the self-consistency literature) — G2G points it at derivations instead of answers. Divergence between independent runs is *information*. Before deducing, each agent performs a **falsification-surface inventory**: list, per proposition, what could kill it — a deducer once cancelled its own headline bet in its own caveats section without noticing, and the inventory is the vaccine. Then the **merge protocol**, run by the orchestrator (the planning-seat agent running the cycle, §7): **freeze** the independent briefs first; build a divergence list (who derived what the others missed; who contradicts whom); let each deducer respond *to the list only*, as marked amendments, with the frozen originals preserved for the tribunal; consolidate into a single deduction brief. Independence governs generation; the exchange happens only after every brief is frozen — deducers who can see each other *while deriving* converge, and the entire point is that they must not.

- **P6 Tribunal.** Fresh-context critics with divided mandates: a *logic critic* (internal contradictions only), an *evidence critic* (conflicts with data and history; live search; carrier checks), a *novelty critic* (has this been done — and better?). Two constraints remain mandatory: **anti-sycophancy** (minimum objection quotas; overall endorsement forbidden) and **fresh context per round** — agents sharing one long conversation drift toward agreement, a documented tendency of the models and the one failure this design cannot afford. Quotas carry one escape valve: a critic that cannot reach quota with *receivable* objections says so explicitly — "receivable surface exhausted" — which is itself information; fabricating objections to satisfy a quota is forbidden. v2.0 adds the machinery practice invented:
  - **Severity dial.** Adversarial intensity is a *choice the human makes*, not a constant. Three tiers — **exploratory** (≥3 objections per critic, one defense round, defended by the deducer: for hunches, essays, early-stage ideas), **standard** (≥5, up to two rounds, independent defender optional: the default for working research), **submission** (≥5, up to three rounds, independent defender with data recomputation mandatory, translation-layer check mandatory: for anything leaving the building — outward-bound material is submission-tier material *by definition*). The tier is set before the tribunal sits and is never adjusted mid-proceeding because objections are landing. "Exploratory tone" governs the verdict's framing — risks and open questions rather than a kill list — not the objections themselves, which are never diluted. Full rigor everywhere is not discipline; it is a way to burn tokens and humans. The dial never touches honesty — at every tier, endorsement stays forbidden, evidence stays sourced, and quotas stay enforced.
  - **Exemption rule.** Objections that a *declared assumption is false* go to the silent ledger (§3-P3), uncounted and unlitigated. Objections that the deduction *violates* a declared assumption, or that an operationalization *drifted* from it, are received at full force. The tribunal's proper target is execution, not the human's premises.
  - **Independent defender.** From standard severity up (optional at standard, mandatory at submission), the defense is run by a separate defender agent in a fresh context with **access to the programme's data files**, obliged to *recompute every computable allegation* rather than argue it. Practice earned this seat: a defender re-deriving figures from primary tables repeatedly overturned plausible-sounding numerical objections — and, in the same pass, caught errors on its own side the critics had missed. Defense verdicts use three values: **defended / partly defended / conceded**, one-line reasons, defenses only from inside the axioms and declared assumptions; needing a new axiom means conceding and routing the proposal upward.
  - **Sentence-level verdicts.** Verdicts kill *sentences*, not just claims, and every killed sentence receives **replacement language** in a substitution table that becomes the binding wording baseline for all downstream writing. One precedence rule: wording the human has ratified outranks the table — a verdict may recommend changing ratified wording, but the change takes effect only on re-ratification. Patrol especially the five verbs of overreach practice keeps convicting: *confirm, guarantee, falsify, deliver/vindicate, co-move* — claims whose verb outruns their evidence. Verdict language is three-valued — **alive / dead / undecidable (with the reason)** — and undecidable is not a euphemism for dead: "not detected" must never quietly become "refuted."
  - **Critic collisions.** Independent critics sometimes fire on each other's positions — one critic's "the direction is arithmetically predetermined" felled by another's "the published neighbor found the opposite sign." Collisions are adjudicated *provisionally* by the orchestrator, recorded, and surfaced in the verdict — arbitration proper stays with the human (§2), who can re-decide any collision on review. The record matters in itself: collisions are the adversarial system calibrating, and evidence the isolation is real.
  - **Cross-proposition registration.** A verdict is relative to the proposition as worded. For each major piece of evidence, the verdict registers *what other propositions this number could support or refute* — the same +2.3 vol points that fails a "skew repricing" proposition is flagship support for an "investors do not believe" proposition, and a verdict that buries the number under the first wording wastes the second.
  - **Defense pieces.** Arguments that survive defense get numbered (DP-1, DP-2, …) and registered for reuse in later task briefs — a defense worth making twice is worth writing down once.

- **P7 Grounding.** Each surviving proposition must answer three questions: What observable prediction does it make? What data could refute it? Has history already run the experiment (name the dataset or event)? A claim nothing could refute is decoration, not research — mark it so. Where results enter a frozen registry, sample and specification declarations carry a **programmatic reconciliation** list — machine-checkable counts and ranges — because a frozen regime makes a mis-declared sample a conclusion-level violation even when the arithmetic was honest.

- **P8 Score, stop, remember.** Score the round on **five axes**, 1–5 each: internal consistency, empirical compatibility, falsifiability, novelty, and — new in v2.0 — **sellability**: does this converge on *one* clearly stated contribution, in a shape the target venue publishes, with a story a referee can retell in two sentences? One month of practice was enough to produce results that were evidence-strong, coverage-complete, and unsellable — reviewers buy a point, not a barrage; the axis exists so the loop notices before the referee does. Novelty claims are **pre-coded** — written down before the tribunal sits, so the verdict grades a registered claim rather than a post-hoc gloss. Default pass line: **≥18/25 with falsifiability ≥3 and sellability ≥2**. And the rule that governs all scores: **scores inform; the human disposes.** A card below the line defaults to *archived-in-progress* with explicit **re-scoring triggers** (what evidence, if it lands, reopens the case — practice has twice promoted a failed card past the line on re-scoring); demotion or burial is a human decision, never an automatic one, and an unresolved verdict is never grounds to pile on objections until the human relents. Stop on plateau (two rounds without improvement) or on budget — budget exhaustion is a constraint, not a quality signal. Write conclusion cards and contradiction-log entries *as they occur*; harvest tribunal salvage into the seed registry (mandatory — refutations point at the nearest unoccupied ground); feed both back into P4. The memory, not any single conclusion, is the asset.

## 4. The convergence principle — and the selling point

The publishable — and monetizable — unit of social science is not the grand framework. It is the **identifiable small question**: one mechanism, formalized, that some dataset or natural experiment can actually answer. G2G therefore ends every cycle with a cut: extract the smallest fragment that survived the tribunal and can be identified in data, and develop it to completion. Solved fragments re-enter the framework as known conditions — and usually expose which neighboring parts of the grand story were immature. Grand → ground → grand again, one solved piece at a time. Hence the name.

v2.0 sharpens the cut with two rules practice paid for. **Fit over breadth**: for each link in a mechanism chain, *at most one* registered proposition and one main test — a seventeen-test battery is not rigor, it is a story no referee can retell, and the tribunal's job includes killing test-battery sprawl. **One selling point per paper**: a paper sells one idea; evidence beyond the one idea is robustness, an appendix, or the next paper. The sellability axis (§3-P8) scores it; the plan layer (§6) enforces it.

## 5. Anti-dogma rules

Field experience keeps four mechanical rules that stop the loop from ossifying around the researcher's pet concepts — with one upgrade:

- **R1 Rotation, on two axes.** Never use the same theoretical-framework family in two consecutive cycles — *and* never the same data domain, which practice showed is a second, independent way to ossify (two consecutive cycles in options data taught the lesson). Record the family *and* the domain per cycle in the registry — the genealogy line. Concepts the researcher emphasizes are entry points, not mandates. If the human insists on repeating, comply and log a waiver — the record keeps everyone honest.
- **R2 No re-cutting.** A registered question may not be cut again; derived questions must name their parent and the new increment.
- **R3 Cross-level linkage.** Every accepted conclusion must state what it means for at least one other layer of the framework. A result that is elegant at one layer and meaningless to the others is void.
- **R4 Mechanism first.** Each cycle must propose at least one cross-level mechanism. Describing group differences in preferences is not a mechanism.

## 6. The plan layer

v1.0 treated conclusion cards as the loop's only artifact. Practice grew a second one, and it turned out to be the steering wheel: the **versioned research plan** — plan v1, v2, … vN — the document that translates verdicts into next actions, carries the human's approvals, and briefs whoever executes. v2.0 makes it official.

A plan that works has a definite anatomy. A **plain-language digest** on top — half a page the researcher reads first, no jargon, the "why" before the "what". **Modular work items with registered IDs**: a mandatory repair line (R1…Rn, one item per accepted tribunal finding) and offense lines (A/B/C…, from salvage and seeds) — so approval is a *tick*: the human writes "R1 ✓, R2 ✓, A skip" and the review is done. **Self-containment**: every referenced objection or verdict carries a one-line plain restatement — a plan that says "fix per L8, see tribunal file" has outsourced its own comprehensibility to a document the human will not open; the researcher must be able to review the plan without a decoder. **A decision bar** in the four-line format (§7), each item annotated with **what it blocks** — so the human sees not just what needs deciding but what stalls while it waits. **Version discipline**: approvals are written into the plan file itself, dated; entry-point files never name a version number ("the highest version is current" — a hard-coded round number in an entry file once silently pointed a whole execution round at the previous round's spec); generating vN+1 triggers archiving vN's handover items.

Two gates guard the layer. **The plan tribunal**: before a plan's propositions enter a frozen registry or reach an external collaborator, the *plan itself* stands tribunal — in plan mode, the critics attack the new shapes and mappings the plan adds (its formalizations, its outward translations), not the already-frozen results it cites. Practice's first plan tribunal found fourteen dead sentences, every one in the plan-to-outward-text translation layer — precisely where no gate had stood. Plan tribunals get their own **special series** (ST-01, ST-02, …), never consuming cycle-tribunal numbers. And **plan-first mode**: a programme may legitimately iterate plan → digest → human (or collaborator) feedback → plan, with *no empirical execution at all*, until the frame is agreed — cheaper than running studies nobody has agreed to want.

## 7. Working with the human

The loop's scarcest resource is the researcher's attention. Four disciplines, each bought with logged pain:

**The four-line decision format.** Every item put before the human for decision carries exactly four lines: (1) *what was done* — one sentence of context; (2) *what you must decide* — a concrete choice, never an open question; (3) *the default* — stated as "takes effect unless changed," and genuinely executable; (4) *the cost of not deciding* — one sentence, including what it blocks. Three prohibitions: no "see §X for context" pointers that make the human fetch their own briefing; no defaults omitted (a list without defaults transfers the full decision cost to the human); no undefined jargon inside a decision item. The adequacy standard: the human can decide from this screen alone, without asking the AI anything. The motivating contrast — the same eight decisions took seven round-trips as open questions and one pass in four-line format — is one programme's anecdote, learning effects surely included; the argument is the design: a screen with defaults and consequences is decidable, a list of open questions is homework.

**ID discipline.** Codes are for machines *and* humans: every letter series is registered in a legend at first use (R = repair item, S = seed, C = card, U = ruling, DP = defense piece, ST = special tribunal, …). Revisions take dotted numbers (R2.1), never accumulated primes — by round four, R‴2 is write-only. Any reader should be able to resolve any ID from the programme's front page.

**Session hygiene.** One session, one thing — planning, learning, execution, and writing are four different sessions. Learning sessions (understanding a concept, a test, a result) are opened separately and discarded — they need no project context, and the "teach-back" step is real work: a deliverable the human cannot explain is not yet delivered. Hand off *at* natural boundaries, not at context exhaustion: when the agent starts repeating itself, misremembers an early detail, or a major stage just closed, write the state and open a fresh session. The iron rule beneath all of it: **state lives in files, not in sessions — any decision not written into a file did not happen.**

**The two-seat protocol.** Real programmes run two seats: a **planning seat** (orchestration, G2G cycles, records, human interface) and an **execution seat** (data, code, empirical production) — in practice often two different AI products, but the protocol is seat-based, not product-based. Each seat has a written territory: what it may write, what is its exclusive zone (the planning seat never edits empirical outputs; the execution seat never edits G2G records or prior rounds' directories). Handovers carry a fixed kit — planning → execution: task brief, start prompt, decision defaults, unattended-case dispositions; execution → planning: run report, results reading, objection reconciliation, one log line. Both seats append to a shared chronological log; history is append-only.

## 8. The external interface

Sooner or later the programme meets an outside authority — a supervisor, a co-author, a referee. v2.0 gives the boundary its own rules, all field-tested:

- **Rulings in, verbatim.** An external collaborator's decisions (scope, framing, what they refuse) are archived word-for-word as dated rulings, then treated like standing rulings — argued with the human, not silently re-litigated by agents.
- **Same protection for their intuitions.** A collaborator's spoken story gets the P2 treatment: multi-operationalization, side by side, `[SIG]` marks on every shape the agent adds. Their intuition and the user's intuition have equal rank — the signature dies, not the story.
- **Standard terminology outward.** Internal pet names (the wedge, the floor line, the two-readings trick) never leave the building; outward text uses the field's standard terms. Standing deliverables for the interface: a one-page brief and a call-notes card, both in the collaborator's professional language.
- **The discipline stays invisible.** The internal machinery — frozen registries, tribunal records, substitution tables, contradiction logs, self-audits — runs *inside* and is not outward content. Papers do not showcase their own discipline; self-binding theater adds attack surface, not credibility. This is not concealment: results, nulls, caveats, and data gaps are reported in full — what is withheld is process narrative, never evidence. (The rule governs research outputs; when the process itself is the subject, as in a methodology paper, the process is the evidence and describing it is content, not theater.) **One red line is absolute: a test added after results were known is never described outward as pre-specified. Not mentioning its timing is legitimate; reversing it is fraud. "Not saying" ≠ "saying the reverse."**
- **The step-down rule.** Every outward results sentence is aligned, sentence by sentence, with the frozen verdict wording; qualifiers must survive the translation layer. Practice's plan tribunal found the pattern in one line: the plan carried its caveats and the outward one-pager dropped them — the fix is not a better story but a mechanical alignment pass. Outward-bound material is submission-tier material by definition (§3-P6, Appendix A), so the tribunal runs that pass on it by default — the alignment is part of shipping, not an option.

## 9. Failure modes and guardrails

The five originals: **agent sycophancy and convergence** → role constraints, objection quotas, fresh contexts. **A polluted hard core** → human fact-verification of every axiom — still the single most dangerous failure, because the system will rationalize a false core beautifully. **Context drift in long loops** → external memory files; one round per context window. **Narrative overfitting** → mandatory grounding; falsifiability scored every round. **Human rubber-stamping** → read the tribunal log, not just the conclusion card.

Six more, learned in the field: **Path-dependent deduction** (one deducer, one path, compounding forks) → parallel deduction with late merge. **Naive signatures** (the agent's added shape dies and takes the human's story's reputation with it) → multi-operationalization, `[SIG]` marks, and tribunals that name *whose* addition died. **Translation-layer overselling** (caveats present in the plan, gone in the one-pager) → the step-down rule and the plan tribunal. **Over-negation** (firepower on the human's premises until the human disengages) → the declared-assumption tier, the severity dial, and the responsibility clause of §2. **ID inflation** (R‴2 and friends) → the legend and dotted revisions. **Token blowups** (one session, seven jobs) → session hygiene and the severity dial's economics: spend depth where the stakes are.

## 10. Start today

You do not need infrastructure to start; you need the discipline and a folder of Markdown files. Initialize a programme (state your vision, your layers, your protected core, your declared assumptions, your severity default, your field's conventions). Run a cycle: watch independent deducers disagree, three critics attack in isolation, a defender recompute the numbers, and a verdict hand you exact surviving sentences with a decision bar you can clear in one pass. Draft the plan, tick the modules — and when it goes to your supervisor, it goes at submission severity, so every sentence in the one-pager has passed the alignment against frozen verdict wording. When a card fails, archive it with its re-scoring triggers and move on — the memory, not the conclusion, is the asset. The methodology is the cheap part; the discipline — verify, ground, stop — is the work.

## Lineage

G2G steals openly, and says from whom. The plan tribunal and plan-first mode descend from Registered Reports and the grant-panel tradition — review the plan before the work. Parallel deduction is self-consistency ensembling pointed at derivations instead of answers, run on Delphi's schedule: independence first, controlled exchange after. The declared-assumption tier operationalizes Lakatos's negative heuristic — aim criticism at the belt, not the core — with the assumption-audit habits of intelligence tradecraft bolted on. Three-value verdicts echo claim-verification practice; the severity dial is proportionate review, as old as ethics boards; the red line is the research-integrity consensus on HARKing. The contribution is not any single device. It is the integration: all of it mechanized, quota-enforced, file-backed, and runnable by one researcher with off-the-shelf agents, in disciplines that have no lab to close the loop.

## Appendix B — ID naming rules

Every letter series is registered in the programme legend at first use. Reserved defaults: **C** conclusion cards (C-001…), **S** seeds/questions (S1…), **R** plan repair modules, **A/B/…** plan offense lines, **U** standing rulings, **DP** defense pieces, **ST** special (plan-level) tribunals, **P** pipeline phases, **H/A-n** hard-core axioms. IDs are **scoped by document type**, and cross-document references carry the scope word: "Rule R1" (the methodology's anti-dogma rules, a closed set of four), "plan R2" (a repair module), "axiom A-1" (hard core), "offense line A" (a plan). Within one document type every ID must be resolvable from the legend by a reader with no project history; a series that would collide inside the same document type must be renamed at registration. Revisions use dotted numbers (S13.1, R2.1) — primes (S13′, R″2) are forbidden beyond a single occurrence. Tribunals over cycle output number #01, #02, …; plan tribunals number ST-01, ST-02, … and never consume cycle numbers.
